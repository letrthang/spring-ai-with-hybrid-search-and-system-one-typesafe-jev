# Biotech basics — fermentation and cell culture

## Fermentation
In fermentation, microbes such as yeast or bacteria turn sugar into useful products. Yeast makes ethanol and carbon
dioxide (bread and beer); other microbes make yoghurt, antibiotics, enzymes and insulin. Industrial fermentation runs
in a bioreactor: a steel tank that controls temperature, pH, stirring and air supply.

## Oxygen in a bioreactor
Most production microbes need oxygen. Air is bubbled in and a stirrer breaks the bubbles up so oxygen can dissolve.
If dissolved oxygen falls too low the cells slow down or switch to making by-products, so the bioreactor raises the
stirring speed or the air flow when the oxygen sensor reading drops.

## Growing animal cells
Animal cells are grown in a sterile incubator at 37 °C with 5 % CO2, in a liquid medium kept at pH 7.2–7.4. All work
is done in a laminar-flow hood with sterilised tools, because a single bacterium can overgrow the culture in a day.
