# Physics basics — heat and gases

## Temperature and heat
Temperature measures how fast the particles of a substance move on average; heat is energy flowing from a hotter
object to a colder one. Heating 1 kg of water by 1 °C takes about 4,200 J, which is why water warms up and cools down
slowly.

## The ideal gas law
For a gas, pressure times volume equals the amount of gas times a constant times the absolute temperature:
p V = n R T. Squeeze a gas into half the volume at the same temperature and its pressure doubles; heat it in a closed
container and the pressure rises.

## Gases dissolving in liquids (Henry's law)
The amount of a gas that dissolves in a liquid is proportional to the pressure of that gas above it (Henry's law).
Warm liquids hold less dissolved gas than cold ones: water at 30 °C holds about a quarter less oxygen than water at
10 °C. That is why a fizzy drink goes flat faster when warm, and why fish ponds and bioreactors run short of oxygen in
hot weather.
