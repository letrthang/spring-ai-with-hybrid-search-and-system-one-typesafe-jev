# Biotech basics — DNA and genes

## DNA structure
DNA is a long molecule made of two strands twisted into a double helix. Each strand is a chain of four bases:
adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G) and cytosine (C). A always pairs with T, and G always pairs with C, so one
strand is a mirror copy of the other. A human cell holds about 3 billion base pairs.

## From gene to protein
A gene is a stretch of DNA that carries the recipe for one protein. The cell first copies the gene into messenger
RNA (transcription), then a ribosome reads the RNA three bases at a time and joins the matching amino acids into a
protein (translation). This one-way flow DNA → RNA → protein is called the central dogma of molecular biology.

## Mutations
A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. A point mutation swaps one base for another; an insertion or deletion
adds or removes bases and can shift the whole reading frame. Most mutations are harmless, some break a protein, and a
few help the organism survive — the raw material of evolution.
