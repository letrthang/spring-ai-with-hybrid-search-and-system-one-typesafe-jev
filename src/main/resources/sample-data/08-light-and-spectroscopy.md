# Physics in the lab — light and spectroscopy

## Waves and the spectrum
Light is an electromagnetic wave. Its wavelength sets its colour: violet is about 400 nm, red about 700 nm. Speed
equals frequency times wavelength, c = f · λ, and the speed of light in a vacuum is about 300,000 km per second.

## Absorbance and the Beer–Lambert law
A spectrophotometer shines light of one wavelength through a sample and measures how much is absorbed. The
Beer–Lambert law says absorbance is proportional to concentration: A = ε · c · l. Doubling the concentration doubles
the absorbance, so a measured absorbance tells you how much of a substance is in the sample.

## OD600 — counting bacteria with light
Biologists measure how dense a bacterial culture is by its optical density at 600 nm (OD600). The cells scatter the
light, so a cloudier culture gives a higher OD600. An E. coli culture is usually harvested when OD600 reaches about
0.6, in the middle of its fast-growth phase.
