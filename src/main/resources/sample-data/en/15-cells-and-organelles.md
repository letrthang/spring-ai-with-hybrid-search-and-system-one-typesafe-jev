# Biotech basics — cells and organelles

## What a cell is
A cell is the smallest unit of life that can grow, use energy and copy itself. Every living thing is made of one or
more cells, and every cell comes from an existing cell. A typical animal cell is about 10–30 µm across, too small to
see without a microscope.

## Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, have no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles; their DNA floats in the
cytoplasm. Eukaryotic cells, found in animals, plants and fungi, keep their DNA inside a nucleus and have many
organelles. A bacterium is usually 1–2 µm long, roughly ten times smaller than a eukaryotic cell.

## The cell membrane
The cell membrane is a thin double layer of lipids, called a phospholipid bilayer, that surrounds every cell. It is
about 7–10 nm thick and lets small, uncharged molecules such as oxygen pass, while ions and sugars need protein
channels or pumps. This selective barrier keeps the inside of the cell different from the outside.

## The nucleus
The nucleus is the organelle that stores a eukaryotic cell's DNA and controls which genes are switched on. It is
wrapped in a double membrane with pores that let RNA and proteins move in and out. Inside sits the nucleolus, a dense
region where the parts of ribosomes are assembled.

## Mitochondria
Mitochondria are the organelles that turn food molecules and oxygen into ATP, the cell's energy currency. This
process, called cellular respiration, can make about 30–32 ATP from one glucose molecule. Mitochondria have their own
small circular DNA, a sign that they came from bacteria that were taken up by an ancient cell.

## Ribosomes
Ribosomes are the molecular machines that build proteins by joining amino acids in the order set by messenger RNA.
They are made of RNA and protein and are only about 20–30 nm wide. Ribosomes float free in the cytoplasm or sit on the
rough endoplasmic reticulum, and a busy cell can contain millions of them.

## Endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus
The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a network of membranes where proteins and lipids are made and folded. Rough ER is
studded with ribosomes and handles proteins, while smooth ER makes lipids. Proteins then travel to the Golgi
apparatus, which modifies them, sorts them and packs them into small vesicles for delivery.

## Lysosomes
Lysosomes are small sacs filled with digestive enzymes that break down worn-out parts, food particles and invading
microbes. Their inside is acidic, at about pH 4.5–5, which is the pH their enzymes work best at. If a lysosome leaks,
the enzymes are much less active in the neutral cytoplasm, which protects the rest of the cell.

## Chloroplasts and the plant cell wall
Chloroplasts are the organelles in plant and algal cells that capture light energy and make sugar by
photosynthesis. They contain the green pigment chlorophyll and, like mitochondria, have their own DNA. Plant cells are
also surrounded by a rigid cell wall made mostly of cellulose, which gives them a fixed shape.

## The cytoskeleton
The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibres that gives the cell its shape and lets it move. It has three main
kinds of fibre: actin filaments about 7 nm wide, intermediate filaments about 10 nm wide and microtubules about 25 nm
wide. Microtubules also act as tracks for moving organelles and pull chromosomes apart during cell division.
