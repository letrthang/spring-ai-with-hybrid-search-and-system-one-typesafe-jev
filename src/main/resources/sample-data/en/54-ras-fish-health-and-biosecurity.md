# RAS basics — Fish health and biosecurity

## What biosecurity means in a RAS
Biosecurity in a RAS means all the measures that stop disease-causing organisms from entering the farm or spreading
between its units. Because water is reused, one infected tank can quickly spread a pathogen to the whole system.
Good biosecurity is far cheaper than treating a disease outbreak after it starts.

## Quarantine of new fish and eggs
New fish should spend two to six weeks in a separate quarantine unit with its own water system before joining the
main RAS. During this time staff watch for disease, take samples and treat any problems found. Eggs are usually
disinfected with an iodine bath on arrival.

## Staff hygiene and footbaths
Staff hygiene is one of the most important biosecurity measures, because people carry pathogens on boots, hands and
clothes. RAS farms use footbaths with disinfectant at every entrance, hand washing stations and separate clothing for
each building. Staff should work from the youngest fish to the oldest, never the other way.

## Separate equipment for each unit
Each RAS unit or quarantine area should have its own nets, buckets, brushes and sampling tools, often colour-coded.
Shared equipment moves pathogens from tank to tank. Any equipment that must be shared is cleaned and disinfected
before it moves between units.

## Removing dead fish daily
Dead and dying fish should be removed from every tank at least once a day and recorded by tank and cause. Dead fish
rot quickly in warm water, release ammonia and feed pathogens. A sudden rise in daily mortality is often the first
sign of a disease or water quality problem.

## Common diseases in RAS
Common RAS diseases include gill disease, bacterial infections such as Flavobacterium and Aeromonas, and parasites
like Ichthyophthirius (white spot) and Costia. Shrimp systems must also guard against white spot syndrome virus.
Many of these problems are made worse by poor water quality, high stocking density or stress.

## Signs of sick fish
Sick fish often stop eating, swim slowly or at the surface, flash or rub against tank walls, or gather near the
outlet. Physical signs include clamped fins, pale or swollen gills, sores, fin rot and cloudy eyes. Any of these
signs should be reported and a few fish examined by a vet or fish health specialist.

## Why stress causes disease
Stress weakens the fish immune system, so stressed fish catch diseases they would normally resist. Common stressors
in a RAS are handling, grading, crowding, poor water quality, sudden temperature changes and loud noise. Keeping
conditions stable is one of the best ways to keep fish healthy.

## Treating fish in a RAS
Fish in a RAS are often treated with salt, hydrogen peroxide or formalin baths, and sometimes with prescribed
medicines in feed. Many treatments can harm the biofilter, so they are dosed carefully or given with the biofilter
bypassed. All treatments should follow a vet's advice and local rules.

## Disinfecting a RAS between batches
A RAS is fully disinfected between production batches to break disease cycles. Tanks and pipes are drained, scrubbed,
and treated with a disinfectant such as chlorine, peracetic acid or a high pH, then rinsed and dried. The biofilter
must then be restarted and cycled before new fish arrive.
