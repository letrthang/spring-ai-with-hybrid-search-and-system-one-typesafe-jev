# RAS basics — Mechanical filtration

## Why solids must be removed quickly
Solid waste must be removed from RAS water quickly, because faeces and uneaten feed break down into ammonia and use
up oxygen. Fine particles also clog the biofilter, irritate fish gills and shelter harmful bacteria. The goal is to
take solids out within minutes of leaving the tank, before they crumble into tiny pieces.

## What a drum filter does
A drum filter is a rotating cylinder covered with a fine screen that catches solid particles as water flows through
it. When the screen clogs, the water level inside rises, a level switch starts the drum turning, and spray nozzles
wash the caught solids into a sludge channel. Drum filters are the most common mechanical filter in modern RAS.

## Choosing the micro-screen mesh size
Micro-screen mesh size is the opening size of the drum filter screen, usually between 40 and 100 microns in a RAS.
A 60 micron screen is a common choice for salmon and trout farms. Finer screens catch more solids but backwash more
often, use more wash water and need a larger filter for the same flow.

## Drum filter backwash frequency
A drum filter in normal operation backwashes every few minutes, triggered by the rising water level inside the drum.
A sudden increase in backwash frequency usually means more solids, for example after feeding or when fish are
stressed. Constant backwashing points to a torn screen, blocked nozzles or a flow higher than the filter is built for.

## What a swirl separator does
A swirl separator is a round settling tank that uses a spinning water flow to push heavy particles to the centre of
the bottom. The settled solids are drawn off through a central drain while cleaner water leaves near the top. Swirl
separators are often placed before a drum filter to remove large, heavy waste such as uneaten feed pellets.

## Dual-drain fish tanks
A dual-drain tank has a bottom centre drain that takes about 10 to 20 % of the flow with most of the settled solids,
and a side drain that takes the remaining cleaner water. The circular flow in the tank sweeps waste to the centre
like tea leaves in a stirred cup. This design removes solids fast and reduces the load on the drum filter.

## What a protein skimmer does
A protein skimmer, also called a foam fractionator, removes very fine particles and dissolved organic matter by
bubbling air through a column of water. The organic molecules stick to the bubbles and form a foam that is pushed out
the top. Protein skimmers work best in salt or brackish water, such as in shrimp and marine fish systems.

## Total suspended solids targets
Total suspended solids (TSS) is the weight of particles floating in the water, and a well-run RAS keeps it below 10
to 15 mg/L. High TSS makes the water cloudy, coats the biofilter media and damages fish gills. A rising TSS trend
often
means the drum filter is underperforming or the fish are being overfed.

## Sludge handling in a RAS
Sludge from drum filters and settling tanks is collected in a sludge tank, where it thickens before being removed from
the farm. Many farms dewater the sludge with a belt filter or geotextile bag to cut its volume. The dried sludge can
be
used as fertiliser or fed into a biogas plant.

## Cleaning the drum filter screen
The drum filter screen should be inspected weekly and cleaned with a mild acid or a high-pressure rinse when grease
or biofilm builds up. A fouled screen backwashes constantly even at normal feeding. Torn screen panels must be
replaced
at once, because solids passing through a tear go straight into the biofilter.
