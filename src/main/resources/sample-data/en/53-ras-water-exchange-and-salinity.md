# RAS basics — Water exchange and salinity

## What make-up water is
Make-up water is the new water added to a RAS each day to replace water lost through sludge removal, backwashing,
evaporation and deliberate exchange. It also dilutes nitrate, dissolved organics and other compounds that the
treatment loop cannot remove. Make-up water must be clean, free of pathogens and close to the system temperature.

## Daily water exchange rate
Most RAS farms exchange 5 to 10 % of their total water volume per day, and the most advanced systems as little as
1 to 2 %. Another way to express this is litres of new water per kilogram of feed, typically 300 to 1,000 L/kg.
Lower exchange saves water and heat but lets waste compounds build up.

## Why nitrate builds up
Nitrate builds up in a RAS because it is the end product of the biofilter and nothing in a standard loop removes it.
Daily water exchange is the main way to keep it under control. Most farms keep nitrate-nitrogen below 50 to
100 mg/L, and lower for sensitive salmon smolts.

## Denitrification reactors
A denitrification reactor removes nitrate by letting special bacteria convert it into harmless nitrogen gas in a
low-oxygen chamber. The bacteria need a carbon source such as methanol or sludge from the drum filter. These reactors
let farms cut their daily water exchange to very low levels.

## Treating incoming water
Incoming make-up water is usually filtered, disinfected with UV or ozone, and degassed before it enters the RAS.
Well water may need iron or manganese removal and aeration to strip out excess nitrogen or CO2. Untreated river or
sea water can carry parasites and viruses into the farm.

## Salinity in RAS farming
Salinity is the amount of dissolved salt in water, measured in parts per thousand (ppt) or practical salinity units.
Fresh water is below 0.5 ppt, brackish water is 0.5 to 30 ppt, and sea water is about 35 ppt. Each species has a
preferred range, and salinity should be kept stable within a few ppt.

## Salinity for common RAS species
Rainbow trout and tilapia are grown in fresh water, while barramundi and whiteleg shrimp grow well in brackish water
of 10 to 25 ppt. Atlantic salmon smolts start in fresh water and are moved to brackish or full sea water as they
prepare for life at sea. Many shrimp RAS run at about 15 to 20 ppt.

## Adding salt to a freshwater RAS
Many freshwater RAS farms add a little salt, about 1 to 3 ppt, to reduce stress and protect fish from nitrite
toxicity. The chloride in salt blocks nitrite uptake at the gills. Salt also helps fish keep their body fluids in
balance during handling and transport.

## Changing salinity gradually
Salinity should be changed gradually, usually by no more than 2 to 5 ppt per day, so the fish and the biofilter can
adjust. Nitrifying bacteria are especially sensitive, and a sudden shift can stop nitrification and cause an ammonia
spike. Salmon smolt transfer to sea water follows a planned salinity ramp.

## Measuring salinity
Salinity is measured with a conductivity sensor, which converts electrical conductivity and temperature into ppt.
Handheld refractometers are a quick manual check of the online reading. Conductivity sensors need regular cleaning
and calibration with a standard solution, because fouling makes them read low.
