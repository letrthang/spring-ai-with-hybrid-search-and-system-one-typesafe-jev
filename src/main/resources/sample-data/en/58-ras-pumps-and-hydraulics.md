# RAS basics — Pumps and hydraulics

## The role of pumps in a RAS
Pumps keep water moving through the whole RAS loop, from the fish tanks through the filters and back again, around the
clock. If the main pumps stop, oxygen supply and waste removal stop with them. Pumps are usually the largest single
user of electricity on a RAS farm.

## Total dynamic head
Total dynamic head is the total height, in metres, that a pump must lift water, including the static lift and the
friction losses in pipes, valves and filters. RAS pumps are usually designed for low heads of 2 to 6 m. Every metre
of extra head adds pumping energy for the life of the farm.

## How to size a recirculation pump
A recirculation pump is sized from the required flow rate and the total head at that flow, matched to the pump's
performance curve. The flow is usually set by the oxygen and ammonia needs of the fish. A pump should run near its
best efficiency point to save energy and reduce wear.

## Flow rate and turnover rate
Turnover rate is how many times per hour or day the total system volume passes through the treatment loop. Many RAS
designs turn over the full system volume once every 30 to 60 minutes. The required flow is usually calculated from
oxygen demand or the biofilter's ammonia capacity.

## Variable-speed pumps
Variable-speed pumps use a frequency converter to change pump speed and flow to match demand. Reducing speed by 20 %
cuts power use by roughly half, because pump power rises with the cube of speed. Many farms lower flow at night or
between feedings to save energy.

## Air-lift pumps
An air-lift pump moves water by blowing air into the bottom of a vertical pipe, so the lighter air-water mix rises and
overflows. Air-lifts can lift water only a short height, but they also add oxygen and strip CO2 at the same time.
They are used in some low-head RAS designs.

## Gravity flow design
Many RAS designs use gravity to move water from the tanks through the drum filter and biofilter, with a single pump
lifting it back up. This keeps the total head low and saves energy. Gravity pipes must be sized generously so that
the flow never backs up into the fish tanks.

## Pipe sizing and velocity
Pipes in a RAS are usually sized for water velocities of about 1 to 2 m/s in pressure lines and lower in gravity
lines. Too high a velocity raises friction losses and energy use, while too low a velocity lets solids settle in the
pipe. Smooth, wide bends also reduce head loss.

## Pump cavitation
Cavitation happens when the pressure at a pump inlet falls so low that bubbles form and then collapse violently inside
the pump. It sounds like gravel in the pump and quickly damages the impeller. Common causes are a low sump level, a
blocked inlet or too long a suction pipe.

## Sump level and air entrainment
The sump must stay deep enough above the pump inlet to prevent whirlpools that suck air into the pump. Entrained air
reduces flow and can cause nitrogen supersaturation in the water. Low-level switches stop the pump or raise an alarm
before the sump runs too low.
