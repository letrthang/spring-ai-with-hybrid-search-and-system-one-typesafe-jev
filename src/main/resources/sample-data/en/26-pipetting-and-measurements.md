# Biotech basics — pipetting and measurements

## What a micropipette does
A micropipette measures and moves small, exact volumes of liquid, usually from 0.1 µL to 1,000 µL. The volume is set
with a dial, and a disposable plastic tip holds the liquid so the pipette itself never touches the sample. Common
sizes are P10, P20, P200 and P1000, named after their maximum volume in microlitres.

## Choosing the right pipette
Always choose the smallest pipette that can hold the volume you need. Pipettes are least accurate at the bottom of
their range, so 15 µL is best measured with a P20, not a P200. Never set a pipette above its maximum or below its
minimum, as this can damage it and gives wrong volumes.

## The two stops of a pipette
A micropipette plunger has two stops. To draw liquid, press to the first stop, dip the tip a few millimetres into the
liquid and release slowly. To dispense, press to the first stop, then push on to the second stop to blow out the last
drop.

## Good pipetting technique
Good pipetting technique gives accurate and repeatable volumes. Hold the pipette upright when drawing liquid, release
the plunger slowly and smoothly, and wipe nothing off the tip. Pre-wetting the tip by drawing and releasing the liquid
two or three times improves accuracy, especially for volatile or viscous liquids.

## Reverse pipetting
Reverse pipetting is a technique for viscous or foaming liquids, such as glycerol or detergent solutions. You press
to the second stop to draw liquid, then dispense only to the first stop, leaving a little behind in the tip. This
avoids bubbles and gives a more accurate volume for difficult liquids.

## Metric volume units
Lab volumes are measured in litres and their smaller units. One litre (L) equals 1,000 millilitres (mL), and one
millilitre equals 1,000 microlitres (µL). So 1 µL is one millionth of a litre, and a single drop of water is about
50 µL.

## Molarity
Molarity is the concentration of a solution in moles of solute per litre of solution, written M. A 1 M sodium
chloride solution contains 58.44 g of NaCl in 1 litre, because that is the mass of one mole. Smaller units such as
millimolar (mM, one thousandth of a molar) and micromolar (µM) are common in biology.

## Making a dilution with C1V1 = C2V2
The formula C1V1 = C2V2 calculates how to dilute a stock solution. C1 and V1 are the stock concentration and the
volume you take, and C2 and V2 are the final concentration and volume. To make 100 mL of 1x buffer from a 10x stock,
take 10 mL of stock and add 90 mL of water.

## Serial dilutions
A serial dilution is a series of step-by-step dilutions, each using the previous one as its starting solution. In a
tenfold series, 100 µL of sample goes into 900 µL of diluent, is mixed, and 100 µL of that goes into the next tube.
Six steps give a millionfold dilution, useful for counting bacteria.

## Analytical balances
An analytical balance weighs small masses to about 0.1 mg. It sits on a stable bench, has glass doors to block air
currents, and must be tared, or zeroed, with the empty weighing boat on the pan. Chemicals are never weighed directly
on the pan, and spills are cleaned at once.
