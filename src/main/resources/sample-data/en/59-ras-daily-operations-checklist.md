# RAS basics — Daily operations checklist

## Morning walk-round of the fish tanks
The day in a RAS starts with a walk-round of every tank to check fish behaviour, water level, inlet flow and outlet
screens. Staff look for fish that are gasping, crowding, flashing or not swimming normally. Any unusual sign is
recorded and checked against the sensor readings straight away.

## Daily water quality checks
Each day, operators check DO, temperature and pH on the online system and confirm them with a handheld meter. TAN,
nitrite and CO2 are measured by hand at least once a day, usually after the main feeding peak. Alkalinity and nitrate
are typically tested two or three times a week.

## Daily mortality removal and records
Dead fish are removed from every tank each morning and counted by tank, and the numbers are entered in the farm
records. Unusual numbers or signs of disease are reported to the manager. Removed fish are stored in a sealed
container for safe disposal.

## Daily feeding check
Operators check that every automatic feeder is working, has enough feed and is dispensing the correct daily ration.
They watch fish during at least one feeding to judge appetite and look for uneaten pellets. Feed rations are adjusted
when appetite changes or water quality is poor.

## Checking the drum filter and biofilter
The daily round includes checking that the drum filter backwashes normally and the spray nozzles are clear. The
moving-bed biofilter should show even, rolling movement of the media with no dead zones. Any foam, unusual smell or
change in colour is noted.

## Checking oxygen supply
Operators check the oxygen supply every day, including the liquid oxygen tank level or oxygen generator status, line
pressure and backup cylinder pressure. They confirm that the oxygen cones and backup diffusers are ready to work.
Low oxygen stock should be reordered well before it runs out.

## Checking pumps and blowers
Every day, staff listen to pumps and blowers for unusual noise, feel for vibration and check for leaks. Pump pressure
and flow readings are compared with normal values. Any change can be an early sign of wear, cavitation or a blocked
pipe.

## Topping up buffer and salt stock
The buffer dosing tank, such as sodium bicarbonate solution, should be checked and refilled each day so automatic pH
control never runs dry. Salt stocks and other consumables are checked at the same time. Daily chemical use is
recorded to spot changes in system demand.

## Reviewing alarms and logs
Each day, the shift leader reviews the alarm log and trend charts from the last 24 hours. Repeated alarms, slow trends
and alarms that were acknowledged but not fixed need follow-up. Findings are handed over clearly to the next shift.

## End-of-day handover
At the end of each shift, staff hand over to the next team with a short note on fish condition, water quality, faults
and pending tasks. The night crew must know which alarms are expected and who is on call. A clear handover prevents
problems from being missed.
