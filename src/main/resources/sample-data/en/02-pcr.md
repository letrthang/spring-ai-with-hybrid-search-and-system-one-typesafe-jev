# Biotech basics — PCR (polymerase chain reaction)

## What PCR does
PCR makes millions of copies of one short piece of DNA in a few hours. It needs the DNA template, two primers that
mark the start and end of the piece, the four DNA bases (dNTPs) and a heat-resistant enzyme, Taq polymerase, which
comes from a bacterium that lives in hot springs.

## The three steps of a PCR cycle
Each cycle has three temperature steps:
- Denaturation at 95 °C: the two DNA strands separate.
- Annealing at 50–65 °C: the primers stick to their matching places on the single strands.
- Extension at 72 °C: Taq polymerase builds the new strand, starting from each primer.
One cycle doubles the DNA, so 30 cycles give about a billion copies.

## Real-time PCR and the Ct value
Real-time PCR (qPCR) adds a dye that glows when DNA is made, so the machine can watch the copies grow. The Ct value
is the cycle at which the glow crosses a threshold. A low Ct means there was a lot of target DNA at the start; each
extra cycle means roughly half as much.
