# RAS basics — Oxygenation

## Dissolved oxygen targets in a RAS
Most RAS farms keep dissolved oxygen in the fish tanks between 7 and 10 mg/L, or about 80 to 110 % saturation. Salmon
and trout need at least 7 mg/L at the tank outlet, while tilapia and barramundi cope with a minimum of about 5 mg/L.
The outlet value matters most, because it shows what the fish have left after breathing.

## Why RAS uses pure oxygen instead of air
A RAS uses pure oxygen because air alone cannot supply enough oxygen for the high fish densities in the tanks. Air is
only 21 % oxygen, so aeration can at best bring water to 100 % saturation. Pure oxygen can raise dissolved oxygen
above saturation, letting the farm hold more fish in the same water flow.

## How an oxygen cone works
An oxygen cone, or Speece cone, is a closed cone-shaped tank where water flows downward against rising bubbles of pure
oxygen. Because the cone widens towards the bottom, the water slows and the bubbles stay inside until they dissolve.
Under pressure the cone can raise dissolved oxygen to 30 to 50 mg/L in its outlet water.

## Oxygen diffusers in fish tanks
An oxygen diffuser is a fine ceramic or rubber stone that releases pure oxygen as tiny bubbles directly into the fish
tank. Diffusers are simple and cheap, but some oxygen escapes at the surface before it dissolves. They are often used
as a backup system that switches on automatically when the main oxygen cone fails.

## Low-head oxygenators
A low-head oxygenator (LHO) adds oxygen by letting water fall through a series of chambers filled with pure oxygen
gas. It needs only 0.5 to 1 m of water drop, so it uses much less pumping energy than a pressurised cone. LHOs are
common in trout and salmon RAS with high flows and moderate oxygen needs.

## Oxygen sources: liquid oxygen and generators
RAS farms get pure oxygen either from liquid oxygen (LOX) tanks refilled by truck or from on-site oxygen generators.
An oxygen generator uses pressure swing adsorption to separate oxygen from air, producing gas that is 90 to 95 % pure.
Many farms run a generator for daily use and keep a LOX tank as emergency backup.

## How much oxygen fish consume
Fish use roughly 0.3 to 0.5 kg of oxygen for every kilogram of feed they eat. The biofilter uses another 0.1 to 0.15
kg of oxygen for each kilogram of feed, because nitrification needs oxygen too. Oxygen demand peaks one to three hours
after feeding, when fish digestion is most active.

## Risks of supersaturation
Oxygen supersaturation above about 150 % for long periods can stress fish and damage their gills. Total gas pressure
is even more important: if nitrogen is also supersaturated, fish can develop gas bubble disease, with bubbles in their
fins, eyes and blood. Degassing and careful control of oxygen dosing prevent both problems.

## Controlling oxygen dosing automatically
Automatic oxygen control uses a dissolved oxygen sensor in each tank and a valve that opens or closes the oxygen flow
to keep DO near a set point. Many systems use a simple on/off band, such as open below 8 mg/L and close above
9 mg/L. Proportional control gives steadier values and wastes less oxygen.

## Safety around pure oxygen
Pure oxygen makes materials burn much more easily, so oil, grease and open flames must be kept away from oxygen
pipes, valves and tanks. Only oxygen-rated fittings and lubricants should be used. Rooms with oxygen equipment need
good ventilation and no smoking signs.
