# RAS basics — UV and ozone disinfection

## What a UV steriliser does
A UV steriliser kills or inactivates bacteria, viruses and parasites by passing water close to lamps that emit
ultraviolet light at 254 nm. The light damages the DNA of microbes so they cannot reproduce. In a RAS, the UV unit is
usually the last step before treated water returns to the fish tanks.

## UV dose
UV dose is the amount of ultraviolet energy the water receives, measured in mJ/cm². Most RAS farms design for 30 to
60 mJ/cm² to control common bacteria, while some viruses and parasites need over 100 mJ/cm². The dose depends on
lamp power, flow rate and how clear the water is.

## UV transmittance
UV transmittance (UVT) is the percentage of UV light that passes through 1 cm of water, and it controls how well a UV
steriliser works. Clean RAS water usually has a UVT of 70 to 90 %. Cloudy water, high dissolved organics or a brown
colour lower the UVT and reduce the dose the microbes receive.

## UV lamp and sleeve maintenance
UV lamps lose power as they age and are normally replaced every 9,000 to 12,000 hours, or about once a year. The
quartz sleeves around the lamps must be cleaned regularly, because biofilm and scale block the light. Many units have
a UV intensity sensor that warns when output drops.

## What ozone does in a RAS
Ozone is a strong oxidiser that breaks down dissolved organic matter, fine particles, nitrite and colour in RAS water.
It also kills many microbes. Ozone makes the water clearer, improves UV transmittance and helps protein skimmers work
better, so many farms combine ozone with foam fractionation.

## Ozone dose rates
A typical RAS ozone dose is about 10 to 25 g of ozone for each kilogram of feed given per day. Ozone is made on site
by an ozone generator from dry air or oxygen. Doses are usually controlled by ORP readings rather than set by hand.

## Why residual ozone is dangerous
Residual ozone is extremely toxic to fish, and even 0.01 to 0.02 mg/L in tank water can damage gills. Ozone must react
completely, or be removed, before water reaches the fish. Common ways to destroy leftover ozone are a UV unit, a
degassing column or enough contact time in a reaction tank.

## Using ORP to control ozone
ORP, or oxidation-reduction potential, measures the oxidising power of water in millivolts and is the standard way to
control ozone dosing. Freshwater RAS tanks are usually kept at 250 to 350 mV. Readings above about 400 mV in fish
tanks warn that residual ozone or oxidants may be reaching the fish.

## Ozone safety for staff
Ozone gas is harmful to people, causing headaches, coughing and lung damage, even at low concentrations in the air.
Rooms with ozone generators and contact tanks need an ozone gas detector that alarms and shuts off the generator.
Off-gas from ozone reactors must be destroyed or vented outside.

## Ozone in seawater systems
In seawater RAS, ozone reacts with bromide to form bromate and other oxidants that are toxic to fish and shrimp. These
by-products last longer than ozone itself and are not measured directly by ozone tests. Marine farms therefore use
lower ozone doses and keep ORP lower than freshwater farms.
