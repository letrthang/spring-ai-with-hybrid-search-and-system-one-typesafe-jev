# RAS basics — Protein skimmers and foam fractionation

## How foam fractionation works
Foam fractionation removes waste by letting fine air bubbles rise through a column of water inside a protein
skimmer. Surface-active molecules such as proteins, fats and fatty acids stick to the air-water surface of each
bubble. At the top of the column the bubbles form a stable foam that carries the waste over into a collection cup.

## What a protein skimmer removes
A protein skimmer removes fine particles below about 30 microns, dissolved organic matter and part of the
free-living bacteria in the water. These are the materials a drum filter cannot catch, and over time they turn the
water yellow and cloudy. By lowering the organic load, a protein skimmer also reduces the oxygen demand of
heterotrophic bacteria in the system.

## Why skimmers work better in seawater
Protein skimmers work best in seawater, because salt makes bubbles smaller and stops them from merging, which
creates a dense and stable foam. In freshwater the bubbles are larger and the foam collapses more easily, so
removal is much lower. Freshwater RAS can still use foam fractionation, often combined with ozone, but many
freshwater farms rely on drum filters and water exchange instead.

## Bubble size in a protein skimmer
Smaller bubbles give a protein skimmer more surface area for the same volume of air, so more organic matter is
captured. Bubbles well under 1 mm across are typical, made by a venturi injector, a needle-wheel pump or fine
diffusers. Large coarse bubbles rise too fast, have little surface area and mostly just mix the water.

## Contact time and counter-current flow
Contact time is how long water stays in the reaction column of a protein skimmer, and 1 to 3 minutes is a common
design range. Many skimmers use counter-current flow, where water flows downward while bubbles rise, which
increases contact between bubbles and organic molecules. A taller column gives more contact time but needs more
pump head to feed.

## Adjusting the foam level
The water level in a protein skimmer is adjusted so the foam rises slowly into the collection cup without carrying
water over. Wet, pale and watery foam means the level is too high and clean water is being wasted. Dry, dark and
dense foam that rises steadily shows the skimmer is tuned well, and the setting often needs small changes after
feeding.

## Protein skimmers and ozone
Ozone is often injected into the air supply of a protein skimmer, because the skimmer gives good contact time and
removes the oxidised organic matter. Ozone breaks up large organic molecules and causes microflocculation, so fine
particles clump together and leave in the foam. The water leaving an ozonated skimmer must be checked with ORP
control so no residual ozone reaches the fish.

## Where the skimmer sits in the loop
A protein skimmer is usually placed after the drum filter, treating either the full flow or a side stream taken
from the sump. Many marine RAS treat a side stream sized so the whole system volume passes through the skimmer
several times per day. Placing the skimmer after the drum filter means the skimmer only has to handle the fine
material the drum filter misses.

## Handling foamate from a skimmer
Foamate is the brown liquid that collects in the skimmer cup, and it is rich in organic matter, nitrogen and
phosphorus. Foamate should be drained to the sludge system rather than back into the fish loop. The collection cup
and riser tube should be rinsed daily, because a dirty riser tube stops the foam from rising.

## Why the foam collapses
A protein skimmer stops making foam when oils from feed, cleaning products or medication in the water break the
bubble film. Foam production also drops when the air injector is clogged with salt or biofilm, or when the air flow
is too low. After a new fish batch or heavy feeding, a skimmer can foam much more than usual and needs to be turned
down.
