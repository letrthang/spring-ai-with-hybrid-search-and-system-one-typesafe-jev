# Physics basics — electric fields and capacitors

## Electric charge and Coulomb's law
Electric charge comes in two kinds, positive and negative: like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Coulomb's
law gives the force between two point charges as F = k · q1 · q2 / r², where k ≈ 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² and charge is
measured in coulombs (C). Doubling the distance between two charges cuts the force to a quarter, and one electron
carries a charge of about 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

## What an electric field is
An electric field is the region around a charge where another charge feels a force. The electric field strength is E =
F / q, the force per coulomb on a small positive test charge, measured in newtons per coulomb (N/C) or the equivalent
volts per metre (V/m). In fair weather the electric field near the ground is about 100 V/m, pointing downwards.

## Electric field lines
Electric field lines show the direction in which a positive charge would be pushed: field lines start on positive
charges and end on negative charges. Lines packed close together mean a strong field, and field lines never cross.
Around a single point charge the lines spread out radially, while between two parallel, oppositely charged plates they
run straight and evenly spaced.

## The uniform field between plates
Between two parallel metal plates connected to a voltage, the electric field is nearly uniform and its strength is E =
V / d. A 100 V supply across plates 2 mm apart gives E = 100 V / 0.002 m = 50,000 V/m. Uniform fields like this are
used to steer charged particles, for example in old cathode-ray tube televisions and in industrial inkjet printers
that deflect charged droplets.

## How a capacitor stores charge
A capacitor is two conducting plates separated by an insulator, and charging the capacitor moves electrons from one
plate to the other through the external circuit. One plate ends up with charge +Q and the other with −Q, creating an
electric field and a voltage between them. No current crosses the insulating gap, and the capacitor stores its energy
in the electric field between the plates.

## Capacitance of parallel plates
The capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor is C = ε0 · εr · A / d, where A is the plate area, d the gap, ε0 = 8.85
× 10⁻¹² F/m and εr the relative permittivity of the material between the plates. Larger plates, a smaller gap and a
better dielectric all increase capacitance. Two plates of 1 m² spaced 1 mm apart in air give only about 8.9 nF, which
shows why one farad is a very large unit.

## Dielectrics and breakdown
A dielectric is the insulating material between the plates of a capacitor, and a dielectric raises the capacitance by
a factor equal to its relative permittivity εr. Paper has an εr of about 3, water about 80 and some ceramics over
1,000. Every dielectric has a breakdown field strength above which it conducts and the capacitor fails; for dry air
this is about 3 million V/m, which is how sparks and lightning form.

## Energy stored in a capacitor
The energy stored in a charged capacitor is W = ½ · C · V². A 1,000 µF capacitor charged to 12 V stores ½ × 0.001 F ×
144 V² = 0.072 J. Capacitors can release stored energy very quickly, which is why camera flashes and heart
defibrillators use them; a defibrillator capacitor delivers about 150 to 200 J in roughly 10 milliseconds.

## Charging, discharging and the time constant
When a capacitor charges or discharges through a resistor, the voltage changes quickly at first and then more and more
slowly. The time constant τ = R · C is the time for the capacitor to reach about 63% of full charge, or to fall to
about 37% when discharging. With R = 10 kΩ and C = 100 µF, τ = 1 s, and the capacitor is practically full or empty
after about 5 τ, or 5 s.

## Capacitors in parallel and in series
Capacitors connected in parallel add up directly, C = C1 + C2, so 10 µF and 22 µF in parallel give 32 µF. Capacitors
connected in series combine as 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2, so two 10 µF capacitors in series give only 5 µF. These rules for
capacitors are the opposite of the rules for resistors, where series resistances add and parallel resistances combine
as reciprocals.
