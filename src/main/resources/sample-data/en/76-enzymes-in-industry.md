# Biotech basics — enzymes in industry

## Why industry uses enzymes
Industry uses enzymes because they speed up reactions at mild temperatures and normal pressure, with very few
unwanted by-products. An enzyme process can replace harsh chemicals, high heat or strong acids, which saves energy
and reduces waste. Industrial enzymes are a business worth several billion US dollars a year, used in food,
detergents, textiles, paper, animal feed and biofuels.

## Enzymes in laundry detergents
Laundry detergents contain enzymes that break down stains the way digestive enzymes break down food. Proteases
remove protein stains such as blood and egg, amylases remove starch, and lipases remove grease. Detergent enzymes
have been engineered to work in cool washes of 20–40 °C and at the alkaline pH of washing water, around pH 9–10, so
clothes come clean with less heat.

## Turning starch into sugar
Starch from corn, wheat or cassava is turned into sugar syrups by a chain of enzymes. Heat-stable alpha-amylase
first cuts starch into short pieces at about 90–105 °C, and glucoamylase then splits those pieces into glucose at
about 60 °C. Glucose isomerase can convert part of the glucose into sweeter fructose, giving the high-fructose
syrups used in soft drinks.

## Chymosin and cheese making
Chymosin is the enzyme that makes milk curdle in cheese making. Chymosin cuts one milk protein, kappa-casein, so
the other casein proteins clump into a solid curd that separates from the liquid whey. Chymosin once came only from
rennet taken from calves' stomachs, but recombinant chymosin made by microbes carrying the calf gene was approved
in 1990 and is now used for most cheese in many countries.

## Lactase and lactose-free milk
Lactase is the enzyme that splits lactose, the sugar in milk, into glucose and galactose. Many adults make little
lactase in their gut, so undigested lactose causes bloating and discomfort. Dairies add lactase from yeast or
moulds to milk to make lactose-free milk, which tastes slightly sweeter because glucose and galactose taste sweeter
than lactose.

## Pectinase in fruit juice
Pectinase is an enzyme that breaks down pectin, the glue-like substance that holds plant cell walls together.
Adding pectinase to crushed apples or grapes releases more juice from the fruit, raising the yield, and breaks up
the cloudy haze so the juice can be filtered clear. The same enzyme also helps winemakers extract colour and juice
from grapes.

## Cellulases and biofuels
Cellulases are enzymes that break cellulose, the main material of plant cell walls, into glucose. In
second-generation biofuel plants, cellulases turn straw, corn stalks or wood chips into sugar, which yeast then
ferments into ethanol. Cellulose is tough and tightly packed, so this step needs a mixture of several cellulases,
often made by the fungus Trichoderma reesei, and is still a major cost.

## Enzymes in textiles and paper
Enzymes in textiles and paper making replace harsh chemical steps. Cellulases give denim jeans a soft, worn
"stone-washed" look by gently eating away surface fibres, so fewer pumice stones are needed, which damage machines.
In paper mills, xylanases loosen the hemicellulose in wood pulp so less chlorine-based bleach is needed to make
white paper.

## How industrial enzymes are produced
Most industrial enzymes are produced by growing microbes in large fermentation tanks that hold 100,000 litres or
more. Fungi such as Aspergillus niger and Trichoderma reesei and bacteria such as Bacillus subtilis secrete the
enzyme into the liquid, which makes it easy to collect. The gene for a useful enzyme is often moved into one of
these workhorse microbes and improved by protein engineering before production.

## Immobilised enzymes
An immobilised enzyme is an enzyme fixed onto beads, fibres or a gel so it stays in place while the reaction liquid
flows past. Immobilised enzymes can be packed into a column and reused for weeks or months, and the product comes
out free of enzyme. Glucose isomerase used to make high-fructose syrup is a classic example: one column can run
continuously for many months.
