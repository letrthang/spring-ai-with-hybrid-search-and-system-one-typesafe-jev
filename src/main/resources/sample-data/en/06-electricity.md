# Physics basics — electricity

## Ohm's law
The current through a wire is the voltage divided by the resistance: I = V / R, or V = I · R. Voltage is measured in
volts, current in amperes and resistance in ohms (Ω). Doubling the voltage across the same resistor doubles the current.

## Electric power
Power is the rate at which electrical energy is used: P = V · I, in watts. A 2,000 W kettle on a 230 V supply draws
about 8.7 A. Energy used is power times time; electricity bills count it in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

## Series and parallel circuits
In a series circuit the same current flows through every part and the resistances add up. In a parallel circuit each
branch gets the full voltage, so one broken lamp does not switch off the others — that is how house wiring is built.
