# Biotech basics — plant biotechnology

## What plant biotechnology is
Plant biotechnology is the use of lab methods to study, improve and multiply plants. It includes growing plants from
small pieces of tissue, adding or editing genes and using DNA markers to guide breeding. It aims at higher yields,
resistance to pests and drought, and better nutrition.

## Plant tissue culture
Plant tissue culture is growing plant cells, tissues or organs on a sterile nutrient medium in a closed container.
The most common base is Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium, with about 3% sucrose and set with agar. Cultures are usually
kept at about 25 °C under a 16-hour light period.

## Totipotency
Totipotency is the ability of a single plant cell to grow into a complete new plant. Many plant cells keep this
ability throughout life, unlike most animal cells. It is the reason a small piece of leaf, stem or root can be
regenerated into a whole plant in tissue culture.

## Plant hormones in culture
The balance of two plant hormones, auxin and cytokinin, controls what a tissue culture grows into. A high
auxin-to-cytokinin ratio favours roots, and a high cytokinin-to-auxin ratio favours shoots. Roughly equal amounts
produce callus, an unorganised mass of dividing cells.

## Micropropagation
Micropropagation is the rapid multiplication of plants by tissue culture. One shoot tip can give thousands of
identical plants in a few months, all genetic copies of the parent. It is widely used for bananas, orchids, potatoes
and strawberries, and starting from the shoot tip can also produce virus-free plants.

## Agrobacterium transformation
Agrobacterium tumefaciens is a soil bacterium that naturally transfers a piece of its DNA into plant cells. Scientists
replace the bacterium's tumour-causing genes with a useful gene, so the bacterium delivers that gene into the plant's
chromosomes instead. It is the most common way to make genetically modified plants.

## The gene gun
The gene gun, or biolistic method, shoots tiny gold or tungsten particles coated with DNA into plant cells. The
particles are about 1 µm across and are fired by a burst of high-pressure helium. It is useful for plants such as some
cereals that Agrobacterium does not infect well.

## Bt crops
Bt crops carry a gene from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis that makes a protein toxic to certain insect
pests. The Bt protein is harmless to humans because it needs the alkaline gut and specific receptors found in those
insects. Bt maize and Bt cotton greatly reduce the need for insecticide sprays.

## Herbicide-tolerant crops
Herbicide-tolerant crops carry a gene that lets them survive a specific weedkiller. For example, glyphosate-tolerant
soybeans make a version of the EPSPS enzyme that glyphosate cannot block. Farmers can then spray the field to kill
weeds without harming the crop.

## Golden Rice
Golden Rice is a genetically modified rice that makes beta-carotene, which the body turns into vitamin A. It carries
two added genes, one from maize and one from a soil bacterium, that complete the beta-carotene pathway in the grain.
The beta-carotene gives the rice its yellow-gold colour.
