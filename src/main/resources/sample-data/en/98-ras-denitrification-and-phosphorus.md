# RAS basics — Denitrification and phosphorus removal

## Why remove nitrate from a RAS
Nitrate must be removed from a RAS with very low water exchange, because nitrate keeps accumulating and can reach
several hundred mg/L. High nitrate levels over long periods can harm sensitive species such as salmon smolts and
shrimp. Many countries also limit how much nitrogen a farm may discharge, so denitrification helps meet permits
while saving water.

## How denitrification works
Denitrification is a process in which heterotrophic bacteria use nitrate instead of oxygen to break down organic
carbon, turning the nitrate step by step into nitrogen gas. The steps run from nitrate to nitrite, nitric oxide
(NO) and nitrous oxide (N2O), ending as N2 that escapes to the air. Denitrification also gives back about 3.57 g of
alkalinity as CaCO3 per gram of nitrate nitrogen removed, half of what nitrification uses.

## Carbon sources for denitrification
Denitrifying bacteria need an organic carbon source, such as methanol, ethanol, acetate or glucose dosed into the
reactor. A common rule is about 3 g of methanol per gram of nitrate nitrogen removed, including the extra needed to
use up any oxygen. Some farms use fish sludge from the drum filter as a free carbon source, which also reduces the
amount of sludge to dispose of.

## Keeping a denitrification reactor anoxic
A denitrification reactor must be kept anoxic, with dissolved oxygen below about 0.5 mg/L, because bacteria always
use oxygen first when it is present. The reactor therefore treats only a small side stream at a slow flow, giving a
retention time of hours. Any oxygen that enters the denitrification reactor wastes carbon and slows nitrate
removal.

## Hydrogen sulfide risk in denitrification
Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) can form in a denitrification reactor when nitrate runs out and sulfate-reducing bacteria
take over. H2S is extremely toxic to fish even at a few micrograms per litre, and the risk is highest in seawater,
which is rich in sulfate. Farms control the reactor with ORP, keep some nitrate in the outflow and avoid overdosing
carbon.

## Types of denitrification reactors
Common denitrification reactors in RAS are fixed-bed reactors filled with media, anoxic moving-bed reactors and
upflow sludge blanket reactors that use fish sludge as carbon. A moving-bed denitrification reactor works like an
MBBR but is mixed by a slow stirrer instead of air. The treated water usually returns to the system upstream of the
nitrifying biofilter.

## Nitrite leaking from a denitrification reactor
A denitrification reactor can release nitrite when the process stops halfway, for example because of too little
carbon or too short a retention time. Nitrite in the reactor outflow is toxic to fish, so the outflow should pass
through the nitrifying biofilter before reaching the tanks. Measuring nitrate and nitrite at the reactor outlet
each week shows whether the carbon dose is right.

## Where phosphorus in a RAS comes from
Phosphorus in a RAS comes almost entirely from fish feed, and fish keep only about 20 to 40 % of the phosphorus
they eat. The rest leaves in faeces as particles or dissolves in the water as orthophosphate. Orthophosphate is not
very toxic to fish, but it builds up in low-exchange systems and causes algae growth wherever the discharge water
ends up.

## Removing phosphorus with the solids
Most phosphorus in a RAS is bound in faeces, so fast removal of solids is the cheapest way to remove phosphorus.
Sludge left in tanks, pipes or sludge storage for long periods releases phosphate back into the water. Good drum
filtration and frequent sludge removal can capture a large share of the phosphorus excreted by the fish.

## Chemical phosphorus precipitation
Dissolved phosphate can be removed from RAS discharge water by dosing iron or aluminium salts, such as ferric
chloride or alum, which form insoluble phosphate particles. The particles are then settled or filtered out and
leave with the sludge. Chemical precipitation is done in the wastewater line, never in the fish loop, because the
chemicals change pH and can harm fish.
