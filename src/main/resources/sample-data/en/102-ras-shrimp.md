# RAS basics — Shrimp in recirculating systems

## Which shrimp are grown in RAS
The main shrimp species grown in RAS is the Pacific white shrimp, Penaeus vannamei (also called Litopenaeus
vannamei). Pacific white shrimp grow fast, tolerate a wide salinity range and accept high stocking densities. Shrimp
RAS are often built inland close to cities, so fresh, never-frozen shrimp can reach buyers within a day.

## Salinity for shrimp in RAS
Pacific white shrimp can be grown in a RAS at salinities from about 5 to 35 ppt, but many farms run at about 15 to 20
ppt. At low salinity the water must also contain enough potassium and magnesium, because shrimp need the right
balance of ions, not just sodium chloride. Using a marine salt mix instead of plain salt keeps this ion balance
correct.

## Water temperature for shrimp
Pacific white shrimp grow best at a water temperature of about 28 to 30 °C. Growth slows clearly below about 24 °C,
and shrimp stop feeding well below about 20 °C. Because shrimp RAS run warm, insulated buildings and heat pumps are
important to keep energy costs down in cooler climates.

## Stocking density for shrimp
Intensive shrimp RAS usually stock about 200 to 500 shrimp per cubic metre of water. Final biomass at harvest is
typically about 3 to 6 kg/m³, much lower than for fish, because shrimp live on the tank floor and walls rather than
in the whole water column. Some farms add vertical nets or screens to give the shrimp more surface area.

## Postlarvae and the nursery phase
Shrimp farms stock postlarvae, often at stage PL10 to PL15, which should come from a hatchery certified as specific
pathogen free (SPF). The postlarvae are first grown in a nursery tank for about 3 to 5 weeks until they weigh about
0.5 to 1 g. A separate nursery makes better use of the grow-out tanks and lets the farm count healthy juveniles
before stocking.

## Feeding shrimp in RAS
Shrimp feed slowly from the tank floor, so they are given sinking pellets with about 35 to 40 % protein in many small
meals, often 4 to 8 times a day or by belt feeders. Feed trays are checked to see how much feed is left and to adjust
the next ration. A well-run shrimp RAS reaches a feed conversion ratio of about 1.2 to 1.6.

## Moulting and alkalinity
Shrimp grow by moulting, shedding the old shell and quickly hardening a new one with minerals taken from the water.
Shrimp RAS therefore keep alkalinity at about 150 to 200 mg/L as CaCO3 and enough calcium and magnesium in the water.
Freshly moulted shrimp are soft and can be eaten by tank mates, so stable conditions and good feeding reduce
cannibalism.

## Biofloc and clear-water shrimp systems
Shrimp RAS are built either as clear-water systems with an external biofilter or as biofloc systems. In a biofloc
system, a carbon source such as molasses is added so bacteria in the water convert ammonia into flocs that the shrimp
can also eat. Biofloc systems need strong aeration and careful control of suspended solids, while clear-water systems
behave more like a fish RAS.

## Shrimp diseases and biosecurity
The most serious shrimp diseases include white spot syndrome virus (WSSV), acute hepatopancreatic necrosis disease
(AHPND) caused by Vibrio bacteria, and the microsporidian EHP. Shrimp RAS limit these risks by buying SPF postlarvae,
testing new batches with PCR and treating incoming water. Disease can wipe out a whole shrimp tank within days, so
strict biosecurity is essential.

## Harvesting shrimp
Pacific white shrimp in RAS usually reach a harvest size of about 18 to 30 g after about 3 to 5 months of grow-out.
Survival from stocking to harvest in a good shrimp RAS is often about 70 to 85 %. At harvest the shrimp are drained
or netted out and chilled at once in ice slurry to keep their quality.
