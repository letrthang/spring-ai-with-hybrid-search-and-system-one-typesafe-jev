# RAS basics — Drum filters in detail

## How a drum filter is built
A drum filter is built from a horizontal rotating drum covered with screen panels, a gear motor that turns the
drum, a spray bar with nozzles above it and a sludge trough inside the drum. A level sensor measures the water on
both sides of the screen, and a tank or housing holds the whole unit. Most RAS drum filters are made of stainless
steel or plastic so they resist corrosion in fresh and salt water.

## Inside-out flow through a drum filter
In a drum filter, water enters the open end of the drum and flows from the inside to the outside through the
screen. Solid particles stay on the inner surface of the screen, so they never reach the clean side. When the drum
turns, the dirty screen panels rise above the water, where the spray bar washes the solids off from the outside
into the sludge trough inside the drum.

## Sizing a drum filter for flow
A drum filter must be sized for the peak water flow and the peak solids load, which usually come shortly after
feeding. Filter capacity falls as the mesh gets finer and as suspended solids rise, so a drum filter rated for
clean water is too small for a real fish farm. Designers usually add a safety margin of 20 to 30 % so the filter
does not backwash constantly at peak load.

## Screen materials for drum filters
Drum filter screens are usually woven polyester or stainless steel mesh fixed in removable panels. Polyester
screens are cheaper and common for mesh sizes of 40 to 100 microns, while stainless steel screens last longer and
handle high-pressure cleaning better. Keeping a set of spare screen panels on site lets staff replace a torn panel
within minutes.

## Spray bar and wash water
The spray bar of a drum filter cleans the screen with water at a pressure of about 5 to 8 bar from a small
high-pressure wash pump. Most farms use filtered water from the clean side of the drum filter as wash water, so a
fine strainer in the wash line is needed to keep the nozzles from clogging. A well-tuned drum filter uses wash
water equal to well under 1 % of the treated flow.

## How backwash is triggered
A drum filter backwash is triggered when the water level difference between the inside and outside of the drum
reaches a set point, typically a few centimetres. As solids build up on the screen, less water passes and the
inside level rises until the controller starts the drum and the wash pump. Many controllers also run a timed
backwash every 15 to 30 minutes so biofilm does not grow on an idle screen.

## Drum filter removal efficiency
A drum filter removes most particles larger than its mesh size and, in practice, about 40 to 70 % of the total
suspended solids in a single pass. A thin layer of trapped solids on the screen also catches some smaller
particles. Very fine particles below about 20 microns pass through the drum filter and must be handled by foam
fractionation, ozone or water exchange.

## Seals, bearings and drive maintenance
The rubber seals at the inlet of a drum filter stop dirty water from slipping past the screen, and worn seals let
unfiltered water reach the biofilter. Staff should inspect the seals, bearings and drive chain or gear motor every
month and keep spare seals in stock. A drum that turns unevenly or makes new noises usually points to a worn
bearing or a slipping drive.

## Drum filter overflow and bypass
A drum filter has an emergency overflow that lets water bypass the screen when the drum cannot keep up, so the fish
tanks do not flood. Water passing through the overflow carries unfiltered solids straight to the biofilter. A drum
filter overflow should therefore trigger an alarm, and staff should find the cause, such as a stopped motor, a
failed wash pump or a blinded screen.

## Where the drum filter sits in the loop
The drum filter is normally the first treatment step after the fish tanks, fed by gravity from the tank side
drains. Placing the drum filter before any pump keeps the solids whole, because a pump impeller would smash them
into fine particles. Clean water from the drum filter then flows on to the biofilter, degasser and oxygenation
steps.
