# RAS basics — Off-flavour and purging

## What off-flavour is
Off-flavour is an earthy or muddy taste in farmed fish that makes the fish unpleasant to eat. Off-flavour is mainly
caused by two compounds, geosmin and 2-methylisoborneol (MIB), which bacteria and algae release into the water.
Off-flavour is one of the most common quality complaints about fish from RAS farms.

## Which organisms produce geosmin and MIB
Geosmin and MIB are produced mainly by actinomycete bacteria such as Streptomyces and by some cyanobacteria
(blue-green algae). In a RAS these organisms grow in biofilms on tank walls, pipes, sumps and biofilter media, and in
settled sludge. Areas with low flow and a build-up of organic matter give them the best conditions.

## How fish take up off-flavour
Fish take up geosmin and MIB through the gills, and the compounds collect in the fat of the fish. Uptake is fast and
can taint fish within hours to days, while removal is slow. Fatty fish such as salmon and trout therefore hold more
off-flavour and take longer to clean than lean fish.

## How little geosmin people can taste
People can taste geosmin and MIB at extremely low levels, below about 1 µg/kg in fish flesh. In water, people can
smell geosmin at only a few nanograms per litre (ng/L). These low levels mean even a small growth of biofilm in a RAS
can be enough to taint fish.

## What purging is
Purging, also called depuration, is the practice of moving fish to clean water without feed for several days before
harvest. During purging the fish slowly release geosmin and MIB through the gills into the clean water. Purging also
empties the gut, which improves hygiene and shelf life when the fish are processed.

## How long purging takes
Purging usually lasts about 5 to 10 days, but fatty fish, high starting levels or cold water can need two weeks or
more. Warmer water speeds up purging because the fish metabolism and blood flow are higher. Farms test the fish
during purging and harvest when the off-flavour is gone, rather than following a fixed number of days.

## Setting up purge tanks
Purge tanks should be separate from the main RAS and receive clean water with no geosmin or MIB, often from a
borehole or a well-treated make-up supply. Purge tanks are cleaned and disinfected before each use, because biofilm
in a purge tank can taint fish again. Many farms run purge tanks with a high water exchange or a small treatment loop
with activated carbon or ozone.

## Weight loss during purging
Fish lose weight during purging because they are not fed, typically about 0.2 to 0.5 % of body weight per day. Over a
7-day purge this can mean a loss of several percent of the harvest biomass. Farms balance this weight loss against
the need for clean-tasting fish, so the shortest purge that removes the off-flavour is best.

## Testing fish for off-flavour
Fish are tested for off-flavour by cooking a small fillet sample, often in a microwave, and having trained staff
smell and taste it. Laboratories can also measure geosmin and MIB in fish flesh and water by gas chromatography with
mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Testing both before and during purging tells the farm when the fish are ready to harvest.

## Preventing off-flavour in a RAS
Off-flavour is prevented by limiting the places where geosmin-producing organisms can grow. Good practice includes
fast solids removal, regular sludge draining, cleaning sumps and pipes, avoiding dead zones with low flow and keeping
light out of tanks to stop algae. Ozone can help by lowering organic matter in the water, although it does not remove
all geosmin.
