# Biotech basics — CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing

## How Cas9 cuts DNA
CRISPR-Cas9 is a pair of molecular scissors. The Cas9 enzyme is guided by a short guide RNA (gRNA) of about 20
bases that matches the DNA to be cut. Cas9 only cuts where the target is followed by a short PAM sequence, NGG for
the common Cas9 from Streptococcus pyogenes. Without the NGG PAM next to the target, Cas9 does not cut.

## Repairing the cut
After Cas9 cuts both strands, the cell repairs the break. Non-homologous end joining glues the ends back together and
often adds or removes a few bases, which switches the gene off (a knock-out). If the scientist also supplies a repair
template, homology-directed repair can copy it in and make a precise change (a knock-in).

## Uses and limits
CRISPR is used to study what genes do, to breed crops that resist disease and to develop treatments for inherited
blood disorders. Its main risk is off-target cutting at places that look like the target, so guide RNAs are chosen
carefully.
