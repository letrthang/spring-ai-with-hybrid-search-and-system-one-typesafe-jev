# RAS basics — Troubleshooting common faults

## Rising ammonia or nitrite
A sudden rise in TAN or nitrite usually means the biofilter is overloaded or damaged, often after overfeeding, a
chemical treatment or a drop in oxygen or pH. The first steps are to reduce or stop feeding, check biofilter oxygen and
alkalinity, and increase water exchange. Adding 1 to 3 ppt of salt protects freshwater fish from nitrite.

## Falling dissolved oxygen in a tank
Falling DO in a single tank usually points to a local fault, such as a blocked oxygen line, a faulty valve or a fouled
sensor. If DO falls in all tanks together, the cause is more likely the oxygen supply, the main pump or a feeding peak.
Open the backup oxygen first, then find the cause.

## Cloudy or foamy water
Cloudy water is usually caused by fine suspended solids from overfeeding, a torn drum filter screen or a bacterial
bloom. Heavy foam points to high dissolved organic matter. The fixes are to reduce feeding, check the drum filter
screen, run the protein skimmer or ozone, and increase water exchange.

## Fish crowding at the inlet
Fish crowding at the water inlet or gasping at the surface usually signals low oxygen, high CO2 or a gill problem.
Check DO and CO2 at once, open backup oxygen and stop feeding. If water quality is normal, examine a few fish for gill
disease or parasites.
