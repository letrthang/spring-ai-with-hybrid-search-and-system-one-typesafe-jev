# RAS basics — Feeding and growth

## Daily feeding rate as % body weight
The daily feeding rate in a RAS is usually set as a percentage of total fish biomass, from about 3 to 5 % for small
fingerlings down to 0.8 to 1.5 % for large fish. Rates come from feed tables that depend on fish size and water
temperature. The tables are adjusted to fish appetite each day.

## Feed conversion ratio
Feed conversion ratio (FCR) is the kilograms of feed given divided by the kilograms of fish weight gained. Well-run
RAS farms achieve an FCR of about 0.9 to 1.2 for salmon and trout, and 1.2 to 1.6 for tilapia and barramundi. A
rising FCR means feed is being wasted or the fish are unwell.

## Why overfeeding is dangerous in a RAS
Overfeeding is dangerous in a RAS because uneaten feed rots and adds ammonia, solids and oxygen demand to the water.
It overloads the drum filter and biofilter and can cause ammonia or nitrite spikes. Wasted feed is also the largest
avoidable cost on most fish farms.

## The feed load of a RAS
A RAS is designed for a maximum daily feed load in kilograms of feed per day, because feed drives every treatment
step. Oxygen demand, CO2 production, ammonia and solids all scale with the amount of feed eaten. Feeding more than
the design load overwhelms the system even if the fish are hungry.

## Automatic feeders
Most RAS farms use automatic feeders that spread small portions of feed many times a day on a timer or under computer
control. Feeding in small portions improves feed use and avoids large oxygen and ammonia peaks. Some systems detect
uneaten pellets with sensors or cameras and stop feeding when fish are full.

## Specific growth rate
Specific growth rate (SGR) is the average percentage increase in fish weight per day. It is calculated from the
natural log of final weight minus that of starting weight, divided by the number of days, times 100. Typical SGR
values range from over 3 % per day for small fish to under 1 % for large fish.

## Estimating biomass in a tank
Farms estimate tank biomass by multiplying fish count by average weight, measured by weighing a sample of fish. Some
farms use optical fish counters or camera systems to track weight without handling fish. Accurate biomass is needed
to set correct feed rates and stocking density.

## Grading fish by size
Grading is sorting fish into groups of similar size, usually every few weeks, so that large fish do not outcompete
small ones for feed. Graded groups grow more evenly and have a better FCR. Grading is stressful, so feeding is
stopped for a day before and fish are handled gently.

## When to withhold feed
Feed should be reduced or stopped when dissolved oxygen is low, ammonia or nitrite is high, water temperature is
outside the normal range, or fish are about to be handled or transported. Fish can safely go without food for a day
or two. Feeding into poor water quality only makes the problem worse.

## Feed storage
Fish feed should be stored in a cool, dry, rodent-proof room and used within about three months of production. Heat
and moisture break down vitamins and fats and can cause mould. Damaged or mouldy feed must never be given to fish.
