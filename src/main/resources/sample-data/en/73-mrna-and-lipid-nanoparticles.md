# Biotech basics — mRNA and lipid nanoparticles

## What mRNA does
Messenger RNA (mRNA) carries the instructions for one protein from a gene to the ribosomes of a cell. Ribosomes read
the mRNA three bases at a time, each three-base codon specifying one amino acid, and build the protein in that order.
Natural mRNA lasts only minutes to hours in a cell before it is broken down, so an mRNA medicine makes protein only
for a short time.

## Parts of a therapeutic mRNA
A therapeutic mRNA has five main parts, and each one affects how much protein it makes. At the 5' end there is a cap,
then a 5' untranslated region (UTR), the coding sequence for the protein, a 3' UTR, and finally a poly(A) tail of
about 100 adenosines. The cap and the poly(A) tail protect the mRNA from breakdown and help ribosomes start
translation.

## Making mRNA by in vitro transcription
Therapeutic mRNA is made without cells by in vitro transcription. A linear DNA template containing a promoter is
mixed with T7 RNA polymerase and the four nucleotide building blocks, and the enzyme copies the template into many
RNA molecules in a few hours. The DNA template is then destroyed with the enzyme DNase, and the mRNA is purified, for
example by chromatography.

## Modified nucleosides
Modified nucleosides make synthetic mRNA less likely to set off the body's innate immune alarm. Unmodified RNA made
in a test tube is detected by immune sensors such as Toll-like receptors, which causes inflammation and shuts down
protein production. Replacing every uridine with N1-methylpseudouridine, as in the COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, reduces
this response and increases protein output. The discovery that modified nucleosides calm this immune response was
recognised by the 2023 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

## Codon optimisation
Codon optimisation changes the mRNA sequence without changing the protein it encodes. Most amino acids are encoded by
several synonymous codons, and choosing codons that human cells use efficiently, or that raise the GC content, can
increase protein production and mRNA stability. Designers also avoid sequences that fold into strong hairpins near
the start of the coding sequence, because such hairpins can block ribosomes.

## Why mRNA needs a delivery vehicle
Naked mRNA cannot get inside cells on its own when injected into the body. mRNA is a large, negatively charged
molecule, often 1,000 to 5,000 nucleotides long, that cannot cross the cell membrane and is quickly destroyed by
enzymes called RNases. A delivery vehicle such as a lipid nanoparticle protects the mRNA and carries it into cells.

## Lipid nanoparticle ingredients
A lipid nanoparticle (LNP) for mRNA is built from four kinds of lipid. An ionisable lipid binds the mRNA and later
releases it, a helper phospholipid such as DSPC and cholesterol give the particle its structure, and a small amount
of PEG-lipid controls particle size and stops particles from clumping. The finished particles are usually about 80 to
100 nm across.

## The ionisable lipid
The ionisable lipid is the key ingredient that lets a lipid nanoparticle pick up mRNA and later release it. The
ionisable lipid has an apparent pKa of about 6 to 7, so it is positively charged at acidic pH, where it binds the
negative mRNA, but nearly neutral at blood pH 7.4, which makes it less toxic. Inside the acidic endosome of a cell
the ionisable lipid becomes charged again, disturbs the endosome membrane and lets the mRNA escape into the
cytoplasm.

## Making lipid nanoparticles
Lipid nanoparticles are made by rapidly mixing an ethanol solution of the lipids with an acidic watery solution of
the mRNA. Microfluidic or T-junction mixers combine the two streams in milliseconds, so the lipids self-assemble
around the mRNA and typically encapsulate more than 90% of it. The ethanol is then removed and the buffer changed to
a neutral pH by dialysis or tangential flow filtration.

## Storage of mRNA-LNP products
mRNA-LNP products need cold storage because both the mRNA and the particles slowly degrade. The first COVID-19 mRNA
vaccines were shipped frozen, one at about −70 °C and the other at about −20 °C, and could be kept in a normal
refrigerator at 2 to 8 °C only for a limited time. Freeze-drying with sugars and improved formulations are being
developed to make mRNA medicines easier to store.
