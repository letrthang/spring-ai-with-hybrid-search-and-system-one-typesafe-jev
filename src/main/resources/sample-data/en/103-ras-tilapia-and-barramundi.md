# RAS basics — Tilapia and barramundi

## Why tilapia suits RAS
Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) suits RAS because it tolerates low oxygen, high nitrate, crowding and a wide
range of salinity. Tilapia also grows well on low-cost feeds made mostly from plant proteins. The main drawback is
the low market price of tilapia, which makes it hard for an expensive RAS to earn a profit unless energy and feed
costs are kept low.

## Water temperature for tilapia
Tilapia grow best at a water temperature of about 26 to 30 °C. Feeding and growth fall sharply below about 20 °C, and
most tilapia die if the water falls below about 10 to 12 °C. A tilapia RAS in a cool climate therefore needs a
well-insulated building and reliable heating.

## All-male tilapia
Tilapia farms stock all-male fish because mixed-sex tilapia start breeding at 3 to 6 months and fill the tank with
small fry. Male tilapia also grow faster than females, which spend energy on eggs. All-male stock is produced by
hormone sex reversal in the first weeks of feeding, which is regulated or banned in some countries, or by breeding
with special YY males that give genetically male offspring.

## Growth and density of tilapia
Tilapia in a RAS usually grow from a fingerling of a few grams to a market size of about 500 to 800 g in about 6 to 8
months. Tilapia can be kept at about 60 to 100 kg/m³ when oxygen and solids removal are good. A typical feed
conversion ratio for tilapia in a RAS is about 1.2 to 1.6.

## Tilapia in aquaponics
Tilapia is the most common fish in aquaponics, where nutrient-rich water from the fish tanks feeds plants such as
lettuce and herbs. Tilapia tolerate the nitrate levels and the slightly lower pH of about 6.8 to 7.0 that suit the
plants. The plants take up part of the nitrate, but the fish and the plants must be balanced by matching the feed
load to the growing area.

## What barramundi is
Barramundi (Lates calcarifer), also called Asian seabass, is a high-value white fish grown in RAS in Australia, Asia,
Europe and North America. Barramundi naturally moves between fresh water, estuaries and the sea, so it can be farmed
at any salinity from fresh water to full sea water. Barramundi has a mild, clean taste that sells well as fillets and
whole fish.

## Temperature and salinity for barramundi
Barramundi grows best at a water temperature of about 26 to 30 °C and grows slowly below about 22 °C. Barramundi can
live at 0 to 35 ppt, and many RAS farms use brackish water of about 10 to 20 ppt or full sea water. Salt water helps
control some parasites and lets the farm sell the fish as a marine product.

## Cannibalism in young barramundi
Young barramundi are strongly cannibalistic, and larger fry quickly eat smaller ones in the same tank. Barramundi
nurseries therefore grade the fish every 7 to 10 days until they reach about 10 cm. Feeding often, keeping lights
even and avoiding big size differences in each tank also reduce cannibalism.

## Growth and feeding of barramundi
Barramundi is a carnivore and needs feed with about 45 to 50 % protein, which costs more than tilapia feed. In a RAS,
barramundi usually reaches a plate size of about 400 to 600 g in 6 to 8 months, and fillet fish of 2 to 3 kg in 12 to
18 months. Barramundi are usually kept at about 40 to 80 kg/m³.

## Choosing between tilapia and barramundi
Tilapia is cheaper and easier to raise in a RAS, but barramundi sells for a much higher price per kilogram.
Barramundi needs more expensive feed, better water quality and closer grading, while tilapia tolerates rougher
conditions. Both species need warm water of about 26 to 30 °C, so heating cost and local market prices often decide
which species a RAS farm chooses.
