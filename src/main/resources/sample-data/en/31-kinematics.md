# Physics basics — kinematics

## Distance and displacement
Distance is the total length of the path travelled; displacement is the straight-line change in position, with a
direction. Run once around a 400 m track and your distance is 400 m but your displacement is zero, because you finish
where you started. Distance is a scalar and displacement is a vector.

## Speed and velocity
Speed is distance divided by time, measured in metres per second (m/s). Velocity is speed in a given direction, so a
car going round a bend at a steady 50 km/h has a constant speed but a changing velocity. Average speed is total
distance over total time: 120 km in 2 hours is 60 km/h.

## What acceleration is
Acceleration is how fast velocity changes, measured in metres per second squared (m/s²): a = (v − u) / t. A car going
from 0 to 27 m/s (about 100 km/h) in 9 seconds accelerates at 3 m/s². Slowing down is also acceleration, in the
direction opposite to the motion, and is often called deceleration.

## Equations of uniform acceleration
For constant acceleration, three equations link the starting velocity u, final velocity v, acceleration a, time t and
displacement s: v = u + a t, s = u t + ½ a t², and v² = u² + 2 a s. Knowing any three of these quantities lets you
work out the other two.

## Free fall
An object in free fall accelerates downwards at g = 9.8 m/s², whatever its mass, if air resistance is ignored. Dropped
from rest, it falls about 4.9 m in the first second and 19.6 m in two seconds, using s = ½ g t². After 3 seconds it is
moving at about 29 m/s.

## Distance–time graphs
On a distance–time graph, the slope of the line is the speed. A straight sloping line means constant speed, a
horizontal line means the object is stopped, and a curve that gets steeper means it is speeding up. A line rising 100
m over 20 s shows a speed of 5 m/s.

## Velocity–time graphs
On a velocity–time graph, the slope is the acceleration and the area under the line is the displacement. A car that
speeds up steadily from 0 to 20 m/s in 10 s has a slope of 2 m/s², and it covers the area of the triangle, ½ × 10 × 20
= 100 m.

## Projectile motion
A projectile moves sideways at a constant speed while falling with acceleration g, and the two motions are
independent. A ball thrown horizontally at 10 m/s from a 4.9 m high wall lands after 1 second, 10 m from the foot of
the wall. On flat ground with no air resistance, a 45° launch angle gives the longest range.

## Relative velocity
Relative velocity is the velocity of one object as seen from another moving object. Two cars driving the same way at
100 km/h and 90 km/h close on each other at only 10 km/h, but driving towards each other they close at 190 km/h. A
passenger walking forward at 1 m/s on a train moving at 30 m/s moves at 31 m/s relative to the ground.

## Stopping distance
A vehicle's stopping distance is its thinking distance plus its braking distance. Thinking distance grows in
proportion to speed, while braking distance grows with the square of speed, so doubling the speed roughly quadruples
the braking distance. On a dry road a car at 50 km/h needs about 25 m to stop in total, and at 100 km/h about 75 m.
