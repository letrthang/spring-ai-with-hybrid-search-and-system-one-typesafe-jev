# Biotech basics — buffers and pH

## What pH measures
pH measures how acidic or basic a solution is, on a scale from 0 to 14. It is the negative logarithm of the hydrogen
ion concentration, so each step of one pH unit is a tenfold change. Pure water at 25 °C has pH 7, which is neutral;
lower values are acidic and higher values are basic.

## What a buffer is
A buffer is a solution that resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added. It is made of a weak
acid and its conjugate base, or a weak base and its conjugate acid. The weak acid neutralises added base, and the
conjugate base neutralises added acid.

## pKa and buffering range
The pKa is the pH at which a weak acid is exactly half in its acid form and half in its base form. A buffer works
best within about one pH unit of its pKa. For example, Tris has a pKa of about 8.1 at 25 °C, so it buffers well from
about pH 7 to 9.

## The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation links a buffer's pH to its pKa and the ratio of its two forms: pH = pKa +
log([base]/[acid]). When the base and acid are equal the log term is zero and pH equals pKa. A tenfold excess of base
raises the pH one unit above the pKa.

## Common biological buffers
Common lab buffers include phosphate, Tris, HEPES and acetate. Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at pH 7.4 is used to
wash and dilute cells because it matches body fluids. HEPES, with a pKa near 7.5, is popular in cell culture media,
while acetate suits acidic work around pH 4–5.5.

## Temperature and Tris
The pH of a Tris buffer changes noticeably with temperature, dropping about 0.03 pH units for each 1 °C rise. A Tris
buffer set to pH 8.0 at 25 °C will be about pH 8.6 at 4 °C. For this reason a buffer's pH should be adjusted at the
temperature it will be used at.

## Using a pH meter
A pH meter measures pH with a glass electrode that senses hydrogen ions. Before use it is calibrated with at least
two standard solutions, commonly pH 4, 7 and 10. Between samples the electrode is rinsed with distilled water, and it
is stored wet in storage solution, never dry.

## Making a buffer
To make a buffer, dissolve the buffer compound in about 80% of the final volume of water. Then adjust the pH by
adding a strong acid or base, such as HCl or NaOH, a little at a time while stirring and watching the pH meter.
Finally top up to the final volume with water.

## Buffer capacity
Buffer capacity is how much acid or base a buffer can absorb before its pH changes a lot. It depends mostly on the
buffer concentration: a 100 mM buffer can absorb about ten times more than a 10 mM buffer. Capacity is also highest
when the pH equals the pKa.

## Why pH matters in biology
pH matters in biology because it changes the electric charges on proteins and other molecules. Even a small shift
can change how an enzyme folds and how well it works. Human blood is kept between pH 7.35 and 7.45 by the
bicarbonate buffer system, and values outside this range are dangerous.
